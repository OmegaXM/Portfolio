How it works

From market data to a phone approval

One run does one pass: scan, rank, risk-check, ask a human, then execute. Each stage is its own module, so any one of them can be tested or replaced alone.

1 · Scan

Read the market

Pulls H4 and H1 candles from MetaTrader 5 for each pair.

2 · Detect

Find the setup

Market structure, liquidity pools, order blocks and fair value gaps, then a confirmation pattern.

3 · Signal

Score it

Builds entry, stop and target with a confluence score. Setups are ranked across all pairs.

4 · Risk

Size and validate

Lot size from equity and stop distance, then the risk gates below.

5 · Human

Approve on phone

A Telegram alert opens an approval page. Approve, reject, or let it time out.

6 · Execute

Send and record

Fresh price check, final gate, order, then a durable trade journal.

Safety design

Eight ways the bot is stopped from hurting itself

AI wrote a lot of this code, so the system assumes any single piece could be wrong. Limits are checked in more than one place, and each number is defined once in a shared config.

Two kill switches

Live trading and order execution are separate flags, both off by default. Both must be on before a real order can leave.

Human approval

Every live order needs an explicit tap. No answer within 180 seconds is treated as a rejection.

Daily loss limit

New trades stop once the day's realised loss reaches the cap, read from the broker's own deal history.

Drawdown and size caps

An equity drawdown limit and a hard maximum lot size sit on top of the per-trade risk percentage.

Open-position cap, checked twice

A maximum of 3 simultaneous trades, enforced once before sizing and again just before sending.

Duplicate-position guard

Refuses to open a second position on a symbol that is already open, including one traded by hand.

Stop and margin validation

Checks that stops and targets sit on the correct side of entry and respect the broker's minimum distance, and rejects trades the account's margin cannot support.

Server-side truth

The phone only says which setup to trade. Entry, stop and target are looked up on the server, never trusted from the browser.

What I concluded. The strategy is not proven. Both runs are net negative overall, and each pair has only 6 to 17 trades, which is a small sample. A handful of pairs held up in both runs, but I treat that as a starting point rather than an edge.

Because of that, the bot went live at half the planned risk, on a shortlist of pairs, with a human approving every order. The plan on file is to judge it on live results, not by re-reading the same backtest more hopefully.

Not financial advice. Backtest results do not guarantee live performance.

Operations

Built to run for real, not just in a notebook

Phone app

A home-screen web app shows pending approvals, runs an on-demand scan of all 14 pairs, and lists every executed trade with its realised R.

Alerts and logs

Telegram pushes for approvals, orders and crashes. A rotating log file keeps a full record, including tracebacks.

One-click engine

A launcher starts the approval server, the tunnel and the trading loop. A fallback loop covers machines without admin rights.

Reconciled journal

Every order is matched to the broker's deal history, so the history page shows what happened, not just what was intended.
